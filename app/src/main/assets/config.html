Quick actions

Hold the remote's Netflix button to open these over any app. Each calls a Home Assistant service, e.g. scene.turn_on with {"entity_id": "scene.movie"}. Leave the service blank for a placeholder. Icons use Home Assistant's names, e.g. mdi:movie-open.

Sleep

The last row of quick actions starts a sleep timer: when it runs out, playback stops, the SHIELD sleeps and the TV turns off with it (over HDMI-CEC). Press the row again to cancel.

Music visualizer

Hold play/pause on the remote for Milkdrop visuals (butterchurn) over whatever is playing; hold it again or press Back to close. On the TV: left/right change the preset, up likes (or unlikes) it, down picks one at random, select holds the current one, holding select turns the source images on or off. A short press still plays and pauses, sent on to the app as a media key. Saved changes apply straight away, also while it's showing.

Source images

Pictures fed into the presets for them to melt and mix: each preset warps, zooms and smears the image like anything it drew itself. With several, they take turns in the order shown, and the last one melts away as the next comes in. Steady keeps painting a little in, so the image keeps re-forming as it melts; On the beat splashes it in on bass hits. A PNG with a transparent background (a logo) only adds the opaque parts. A built-in set comes with the launcher; remove any you don't want, and Restore brings them back. Images are uploaded and removed straight away; the other settings apply on Save.

Soft edges fades each image out towards its borders so it blends into the visuals instead of sitting in a box: 0 is a hard edge, 100 a soft oval. Strength is how much goes into the melt, which some presets wash out whatever the strength (they recolour or smear the whole picture). Keep visible also lays the image over the finished picture, where the preset can't touch it: 0 is melt only, about 20–40 keeps it recognisable, 100 is solid.

Rendering

The choices from butterchurnviz.com, less its 2x and 4x canvases. Bigger canvases and meshes are sharper but slower; drop them if the picture stutters.

Ambilight

WLED strips round the room take their colour from the picture: each LED shows the part of the picture it's nearest, as if the picture carried on past its edges into the room. Set the room's size, where the TV hangs, then draw each strip's path on the walls, ceiling, floor or the back of the TV, starting at its data input end. The SHIELD streams to each WLED over UDP (realtime, port 21324, on by default in WLED's Sync settings); when it stops, WLED goes back to what it was doing within two seconds.

Picture

Each LED averages a patch of the picture by the edge in its direction. "How far into the picture" is the patch's depth; "Skip the outer edge" starts it further in, past bright borders, menus or logos at the very edge. Pale, washed-out colour is usually gamma instead: try 2.2 (WS2812 strips are linear, the picture isn't).

Cameras

Double-press the Netflix button to see these over any app. Tick the cameras to show and put them in order; with none ticked, every camera in Home Assistant is shown.

Events that have finished drop off. Read every 5 minutes while the home screen is showing. Reminders

A card pops up in the top-right corner over any app at each of these times before an event in the calendars above starts, with a soft chime that ducks what's playing. All-day events don't get one. It goes by itself after a minute; Back or the Netflix button closes it sooner. Only while the TV is on. Turned on late, the TV shows only the latest reminder due, not every one it missed.

Shows a made-up event on the TV, the shortest time away, with the chime if it's ticked, using the saved settings.

Evening summary

At this time each evening, a card in the same corner lists tomorrow's events, with the chime if it's ticked above. If the TV is off then, it comes when the TV is turned on, until midnight. No card when there's nothing tomorrow. It goes by itself after two minutes.

Reads the calendars and puts the card up on the TV straight away, even if tomorrow is empty.

When the National Weather Service issues an alert for home, a banner crosses the top of the screen over any app, with the alert's text crawling along the bottom, and the EAS attention tone plays once (updates to an alert already out come without the tone). Checked every minute while the TV is on. Back or the Netflix button closes it.

Rain radar

While the Background's weather entity says rain, hail or lightning, or the weather station has seen lightning close by, a small radar pops up in the bottom-left corner over any app (bottom-right over the home screen). Checked every 2 minutes while the TV is on. The Netflix button makes it full screen; Back steps back down and closes it until the weather clears.

Nearby lightning

A strike this close in the last 30 minutes brings the radar up too, even while the weather entity says otherwise. Leave the entities blank to go by the weather alone.

Entities

Holidays

One per line, name: from..to or a single day; the first match wins and replaces the weather image. Days are MM-DD, thanksgiving (fourth Thursday of November) or easter, optionally +N/-N days. A range like 12-31..01-01 wraps over New Year. Names match the image folders.

Backup

Saves this SHIELD's settings to a file: pinned and hidden apps, quick actions, the visualizer and its liked presets, the ambilight's room and strips, room lights, cameras, calendar, weather, background and the Home Assistant address. Not the Home Assistant token: this page never reads it back, so type it again after restoring onto a fresh install. Restore puts back the parts you pick, from this SHIELD's backups or the other one's.
